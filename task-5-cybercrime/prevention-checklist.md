Digital Hygiene & Security Checklist:-

1. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Activate 2FA on your university portals, Gmail, and social media accounts. This ensures that even if someone steals your password, they cannot log in without a secondary code from your phone.

2. Use a Dedicated Password Manager: Avoid using your birthdate or "123456" as a password. Use a manager to generate and store complex, unique passwords for every site you use.

3. Verify Educational Links: Be wary of emails promising "Free Government Laptops" or "Urgent Scholarship Refunds" that ask you to click a link. Always verify such offers on the official university or National Scholarship Portal website.

4. Avoid Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Tasks: Never log into your bank account or university student portal using free Wi-Fi at cafes or railway stations. If necessary, use a trusted VPN or your mobile hotspot.

5. Audit App Permissions: Regularly check which apps on your phone have access to your "Contacts," "Microphone," or "Location." Revoke permissions for apps that don't strictly need them.

6. Secure Your Webcam and Mic: Use a physical webcam cover when not in online classes. Some malware can remotely trigger your camera to record you without the LED indicator turning on.

Financial & UPI Safety Tips (Specific to India)

7. The "PIN is for Sending" Rule: Always remember that you never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money. If someone sends you a QR code and asks you to scan it and enter your PIN to "receive a prize" or "payment," it is a scam.

8. Verify UPI Global/Merchant IDs: Before hitting 'Pay' at a local shop or on a website, double-check the name that appears on the UPI confirmation screen. Scammers often use names similar to popular brands to trick users.

Privacy & Social Media
9. Limit "Oversharing" of Location: Avoid posting real-time "Check-ins" at your hostel or library. It is safer to post photos after you have left the location to prevent stalking or targeted physical crimes.

10. Reverse Image Search for New Contacts: If a "student" from another college reaches out to you out of the blue, perform a reverse image search on their profile picture to ensure they aren't using a stolen photo.

How to Report a Cybercrime in India
* If you or a friend fall victim to an online scam, identity theft, or financial fraud, immediate action is vital to freeze stolen funds:

* Call the National Helpline: Dial 1930 immediately. This is the dedicated 24/7 helpline for reporting financial cyber fraud.

* Official Portal: Register your complaint at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: www.cybercrime.gov.in.

* Local Action: You can also visit the nearest Cyber Cell of your local police station.
